How this is modelled, and what it does not claim.
  • 104 and DNP3 are master-slave. Field devices never talk to each other. Every indication travels up to the master, the master decides, and each switching step needs select, confirm, operate, confirm, one step after another. The master cannot act until the last indication lands, and on a narrow bearer those indications queue behind each other.
  • R-GOOSE publishes once to everyone. Each node adds what it heard to what it measured and works its own switch. No master is in the loop and both isolating switches move together.
  • Ring size moves less than you would expect on a fast link. A single event operates four switches whatever the ring length, and switch travel swamps everything else. Stretch the ring on fibre or 5G and the clock hardly notices. Stretch it on satellite or NB-IoT and the queue of indications starts to hurt. The number that really grows is how many devices have to be healthy and reachable before the master will do anything at all.
  • Static against dynamic. Static self-healing locates the fault, isolates it and reconfigures on topology alone. Dynamic self-healing asks first whether the source taking the load can actually carry it. A polled master cannot answer that from its scan data, because the loading changed the moment the breaker opened, so it has to go and read every device again. Publish and subscribe carries the loading in the same dataset as the status, timestamped and quality flagged, so the check costs no messages.
  • One-way figures used here: fibre 2 ms, private 5G 10 ms, microwave 4 ms, geostationary satellite 250 ms, NB-IoT 5 s. Switch travel is held at 2 s throughout, because no protocol makes a switch move faster.
